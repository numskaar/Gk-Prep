India's latest earth observation satellite: what it does and why it's news

ET
Editorial team · September 4, 2026 Science and tech

India's space programme added another mission to its earth observation fleet this week, expanding the country's ability to monitor agriculture, disaster response, and border areas from orbit. For exam purposes, the launch is a useful anchor point for a cluster of related static GK facts.

Key highlights

The satellite carries a high-resolution imaging payload designed for both civilian and strategic applications. It was placed into a sun-synchronous orbit, which keeps its passes over any given location at a consistent local time — a detail examiners like to test alongside geostationary and polar orbits.

  • Launch vehicle: a medium-lift rocket from the agency's operational fleet
  • Orbit type: sun-synchronous polar orbit
  • Primary use: agriculture monitoring, disaster management, and border surveillance
  • Launch site: the agency's primary spaceport on India's east coast

Why this matters for your exam

Static GK questions on space missions usually test three things: the name and purpose of the satellite, the type of orbit, and the launch vehicle used. Building a simple table for each year's major launches is one of the fastest ways to revise this topic before prelims.
